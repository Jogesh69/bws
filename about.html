Our Story

Behali Wildlife Sanctuary lies in Biswanath district, Assam. It is a patch of semi-evergreen forest in the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas and part of the greater Sonitpur Elephant Reserve. It was declared a reserved forest in 1917, and in May 2022 it was proposed for declaration as a wildlife sanctuary.

The forest lies between Nameri National Park to the west and Kaziranga National Park to the south, so it works as a corridor for elephants. It is an Important Bird Area (1994) and a Key Biodiversity Area (2004).

Geography

About 140 km² at 90 to 110 m above sea level. The Buroi river bounds it on the east and the Borgang on the west. Temperatures range from 13 °C to 37 °C, with about 1800 mm of rain a year.

Habitats

Pristine and secondary forest, riparian forest, grassland and wetland.

Administration

Divisional Forest Officer and range details: to be confirmed by department

History and people

Ruins of a 14th-century town built by the Boro-Barahi kings lie hidden in the forest, along with the Rajgarh rampart and the Maidam Pukhuri tank. Karbi and Munda communities live at the forest edge and use around 100 non-timber forest products.
