Our Story

Behali Wildlife Sanctuary, earlier a Reserved Forest, lies in Biswanath district, in the Behali Constituency of Assam. It is one of the last patches of pristine forest left in northern Assam and is home to a rich variety of wildlife. It is a semi-evergreen forest in the eastern foothills of the Himalayas. The sanctuary protects a valuable forest ecosystem, gives habitat to a wide range of wildlife and helps keep northern Assam ecologically stable.

Special status

  • Key Biodiversity Area
  • Important Bird Area
  • Elephant Reserve
  • Wildlife Sanctuary

Geography

157.6 km². About 90 to 110 m above sea level, with the Buroi river on the east and the Borgang on the west. Temperatures range from 13 °C to 37 °C, with about 1800 mm of rain a year.

Habitats

Pristine and secondary forest, riparian forest, grassland and wetland.

Administration

Range Forest Office, Borgang Range, Biswanath, Assam.

History and people

The British Government declared this forest a Reserved Forest in 1917, by notification No. 4031R dated 13 August 1917, covering 140 km².

Local conservation groups kept asking for stronger protection, Nature’s Bonyapran since 1995 and Wild Heart Foundation since 2014, with people from around the world joining in. In 2022 the Government of Assam added the forest to its protected areas, proposing it as a Wildlife Sanctuary by gazette notification No. FRS.124/2021/386 on 4 May 2022, covering 157.6 km².

Ruins of a 14th-century town built by the Boro-Barahi kings lie hidden in the forest, along with Rajgarh, a rampart attributed to the Ahom king Pratap Singha, and the Maidam Pukhuri tank. Karbi and Munda communities live at the forest edge and use around 100 non-timber forest products.
